# Silicon references & artwork

The profile artwork is a self-contained vector composition. All geometry, materials, PCB routing, bond wires, orbit motion and depth layers are authored in SVG. The hero uses a `1600 × 1020` viewBox, 13.3% taller than the previous stage; there are no scripts, remote fonts, raster images or external asset requests inside the SVG.

The master core floats at `(800, 510)`. It depicts an open semiconductor package: a large iridescent silicon die with four memory arrays and four logic regions, a raised die-attach pedestal, 48 arched gold bond wires, a layered green substrate and two visible banks of silver BGA balls beneath it. The die footprint, package thickness and wire loops are deliberately exaggerated for readability; the pattern is an illustration, not a physical chip floorplan or manufacturing cross-section. The former oversized HK plaque and core caption are removed. The core retains its 9-unit lift over 6.5 seconds, layered onto the shared slow camera pitch.

Four vendor stations follow an inner orbit with base radii `280 × 115`. Layer two is a rigid ten-slot circular formation with base projected radii `520 × 210`: centers stay exactly 36 degrees apart in the orbit plane, and every MCU rotates at 3.75 degrees per second (96 seconds per lap). All angular clocks start together. Native rotation and counter-rotation preserve the formation and keep the models upright. Relative angular offsets and pairwise distances in the orbit plane stay fixed; perspective changes their apparent screen distances. Each chip retains two independently animated local satellites (9–15.3 seconds).

Fourteen wireless, bus and computing capsules counter-rotate uniformly on a 112-second outer orbit with base radii `700 × 360`. A single 32-second camera pitch drives the core and all three rings. It eases from vertical plane compression `0.92` to `0.82` and back, while settling the stage by up to 18 units. Horizontal framing stays at `0.94`. Perspective uses `1 / (1 - k × y / 360)`, with `k` changing from `0.035` to `0.14`: the front grows outward and brighter, while the rear becomes smaller and quieter. Ring outlines, node centers and radial laser links use the same projection. MCU labels and vendor stations counter the camera compression so their text stays upright and undistorted. All fourteen functional cards on the outer ring belong to the orbit plane: their frames, icons and text pitch together with the core and rings, including the same foreground expansion and rear recession. They retain orbital counter-rotation without a billboard that cancels the plane tilt. The core's vertical dimensions are reprojected separately from its top plane, preserving its die walls, wire arches and BGA depth during the pitch. All geometry interpolation is baked at half-second intervals into native SVG animation; no runtime JavaScript is required. Front/back copies share the same clocks and switch at the horizon. Reduced motion freezes the camera and uses static outlines, nodes, energy volumes and core geometry. Independent capsules and satellites can temporarily occlude labels; main chip bodies retain separation.

MCU packages now use a consistent 2:1 projection and a single molded package body. QFN contacts are small terminations at the visible sidewalls; LQFP gull-wing leads have a shoulder, downward bend and flat solder foot in four regular banks. LGA lands remain underneath the rectangular SiP. Pin-one marks are on the top corner. Representative packages are selected from manufacturer documentation; this is an illustration, not a dimensioned CAD footprint. Thermal pads and hidden underside contacts are occluded, and aQFN/LGA underside arrays are simplified.

Each layer-two chip's brand logo forms a luminous energy core whose base, lid opening and upper cap use the original logo vector, including inner cutouts. Translucent side walls follow the logo's outer and inner contours rather than enclosing it in a square. The volume retains the package's 2:1 projection: its base stays at `z=0`, the lid sits at `z=7`, and its upper cap is coplanar with the floating logo. Separately shaded front, side and rear walls, with sparse light columns along their boundaries, give each brand its own volume. The logo rises from 2 to 24 units above the lid and returns over a smooth 4.8-second cycle, with a 0.37-second phase offset between chips. Every wall and column extends with the logo's exact timing and easing, keeping the base fixed and the upper connection continuous. Socket glow and logo brightness peak at the top. Small blur filters apply to the logo and matching socket; package geometry stays sharp. Front/back instances share the same energy clock. Reduced motion shows the complete logo-shaped volume with a fixed 12-unit lift and steady glow. This local energy cycle remains independent of the shared camera pitch and 96-second MCU orbit.

Run `python -B scripts/build_orbit.py` to rebuild the hero from the shared materials in `scripts/orbit-materials.svg` and the exposed core geometry authored by `semiconductor_core()` in the generator. The output embeds all vector geometry and updates the README image hash. The exported SVG embeds the result and has no build-time dependencies at runtime. Side-wall contours are baked in `scripts/logo-energy-contours.json`, sampled from native SVG geometry and simplified with a maximum chord error below 0.02 logo units; caps and bases retain the exact original paths. If those logo paths change, regenerate the contour file with `node scripts/build_logo_contours.cjs` using Playwright and an installed browser, then rebuild the hero. The Python generator checks the source path hashes to reject stale contours. Normal rebuilds require only Python's standard library.

Local Chromium validation sampled 897 instants across 224 seconds, including intervals between baked camera frames. Angular gaps stayed at 36 degrees in the underlying orbit plane (maximum error below 0.000003 degrees), and normalized pairwise distances remained constant (error below 0.0000002). Projected node positions and radial link endpoints stayed within 0.1 SVG units of the shared camera model. MCU labels and vendor stations had negligible skew and scale distortion. Outer functional-card matrices matched the plane compression at every sample, with no camera-facing compensation. Core die height remained consistent with the pitched view. All main chip bodies and capsules stayed inside the canvas, chip body bounding boxes did not intersect, depth layers stayed complementary, and the external-image reduced-motion screenshots were pixel-identical. Rendering speed varies by browser and device. Package shadows remain vector shapes; the brand marks and matching sockets use the small energy glow filter.

## Architecture references

- [ESP32-P4 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-p4_datasheet_en.pdf): dual RISC-V high-performance CPU, rated up to 400MHz; silicon revision and board configuration matter.
- [ESP32-S3 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32_s3_datasheet_en.pdf): Xtensa LX7, vector instruction extensions and memory interface options.
- [ESP32](https://www.espressif.com/en/products/socs/esp32): classic Xtensa LX6 Wi-Fi / Bluetooth SoC; the matrix highlights common dual-core configurations.
- [ESP32-C6](https://www.espressif.com/en/products/socs/esp32-c6): up to 160MHz HP RISC-V core, Wi-Fi 6, BLE and IEEE 802.15.4 for Thread / Zigbee.
- [ESP32-C5](https://www.espressif.com/en/products/socs/esp32-c5): up to 240MHz HP RISC-V core and dual-band 2.4 / 5GHz Wi-Fi 6.
- [ESP32-C3](https://www.espressif.com/en/products/socs/esp32-c3): single RISC-V core up to 160MHz with Wi-Fi and BLE.
- [STM32H7](https://www.st.com/en/microcontrollers-microprocessors/stm32h7-series.html): the 480MHz Cortex-M7 rating applies to supporting parts, rather than every H7 variant.
- [STM32G4](https://www.st.com/en/microcontrollers-microprocessors/stm32g4-series.html): Cortex-M4 with DSP/FPU, up to 170MHz and mixed-signal peripherals.
- The matrix names representative STM32 parts: [H743](https://www.st.com/en/microcontrollers-microprocessors/stm32h743-753.html), [F767](https://www.st.com/en/microcontrollers-microprocessors/stm32f7x7.html), [F407](https://www.st.com/en/microcontrollers-microprocessors/stm32f407-417.html) and [G474](https://www.st.com/en/microcontrollers-microprocessors/stm32g474re.html). Their clock ceilings and peripheral sets are not applied to every member of each series.
- [RP2350](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/rp2350/): two active cores selected from Cortex-M33 and Hazard3 RISC-V CPU options, running at up to 150MHz. The visible label is `2 cores · M33 / Hazard3`; this is not a claim of four concurrently active CPUs.
- [Raspberry Pi microcontroller comparison](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/microcontrollers/microcontroller-chips.html): RP2350 has 520KB SRAM and 12 PIO state machines; RP2040 has 264KB SRAM, 8 PIO state machines and dual Cortex-M0+ cores up to 133MHz.
- [Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi](https://docs.arduino.cc/hardware/giga-r1-wifi/): STM32H747, Cortex-M7 at 480MHz and Cortex-M4 at 240MHz.
- [Arduino Due](https://docs.arduino.cc/hardware/due/): SAM3X8E, Cortex-M3 at 84MHz.
- [ESP-IDF FreeRTOS](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v5.5/esp32s3/api-reference/system/freertos_idf.html): SMP support depends on the target and configuration.

- [STM32H743](https://www.st.com/en/microcontrollers-microprocessors/stm32h743-753.html): single Cortex-M7 up to 480MHz; dual-core H7 configurations belong to other parts. The model identifies FPU and uses RMII and USB HS satellite tags; an external Ethernet PHY is required.
- [STM32G474](https://www.st.com/en/microcontrollers-microprocessors/stm32g474re.html): high-resolution timer with 184ps timing resolution; the graphic highlights motor-control / FOC applications.
- [STM32WB55](https://www.st.com/en/microcontrollers-microprocessors/stm32wb55rg.html): Cortex-M4 plus dedicated Cortex-M0+ radio processor, Bluetooth LE and IEEE 802.15.4. This is distinct from the H743 CPU architecture.
- [nRF5340](https://www.nordicsemi.com/products/nrf5340?lang=en): Cortex-M33 application/network processors and LE Audio; the chip label does not assign a specific Bluetooth version.
- [nRF9160](https://www.nordicsemi.com/Products/nRF9160): LTE-M / NB-IoT cellular SiP with GNSS. The generic outer SIM/eSIM tag does not imply an integrated eSIM in this part.

Outer tags catalogue ecosystem capabilities, not an all-peripherals specification for every illustrated chip. BLE 5.4 and LE Audio identify Bluetooth capabilities whose availability depends on the selected silicon, controller and stack. LoRa requires an appropriate external RF module; the NPU tag can require a companion accelerator. ESP32-P4 has no integrated Wi-Fi radio. USB HS does not describe RP2040/RP2350 USB 1.1, and Ethernet may need an external PHY. Octal-SPI / HyperBus is not a universal throughput claim. CAN-FD 5Mbps and other bus rates are indicative configurations, not application throughput benchmarks.

## Telemetry

The animated Language Mix (`telemetry-3d.svg`) is generated by `scripts/build_telemetry_models.py`. Its compact 1000-unit-wide layout grows to fit up to ten proportional language rails. The Developer Workspace, laptop, terminal and browser models have been removed. Language names and percentages are the only visible data; no activity or streak models/counters remain.

Language logo paths in `scripts/language-icons.svg` are from [Simple Icons](https://github.com/simple-icons/simple-icons/tree/develop/icons), retrieved 2026-10-11, under its [CC0 license](https://github.com/simple-icons/simple-icons/blob/develop/LICENSE.md). Original filenames are the symbol IDs without the `lang-` prefix. The local library includes Python, HTML, CSS, C++, JavaScript, C, TypeScript, OpenJDK, Go, Rust, Ruby, PHP, GNU Bash, Jupyter, Vue, Kotlin, Swift, Dart, Lua, R, CMake and Perl. Icons are tinted for contrast on the dark background; brands retain their trademarks. The fallback code-file icon is authored locally. All selected icons are embedded directly in the output.

Language percentages come from [GitHub Readme Stats](https://github.com/anuraghazra/github-readme-stats) and measure repository code size. Source URL and retrieval time are retained in `telemetry-data.json`. Older saved snapshots can still contain retired activity/streak fields, which are ignored by the renderer.

The UPDATED timestamp records retrieval time, not upstream cache age. The telemetry workflow refreshes every six hours or on manual dispatch. Invalid source responses preserve the last valid snapshot; `--offline` redraws without changing retrieval time. It amends the single root commit on `main`, guarded by force-with-lease. See [the update guide](../docs/telemetry-models.md).

Floating rails, softening shadows and animated rail lighting are decorative. Numeric percentages and horizontal active rail lengths remain fixed until a data refresh. Reduced-motion settings stop animation while retaining the composition. Reflections are clipped to the colored rail surfaces. The SVG has no JavaScript, raster images or external asset requests.
